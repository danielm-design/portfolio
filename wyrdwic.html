WYRDWIC - AT THE HEART OF A NEW WORLD -


Wyrdwic: At the Heart of a New World is a fantasy puzzle management video game with a strong focus on its characters and narrative, created in the Godot engine and inspired by a mix of classic JRPGs and other heroic fantasy works.

As the sole creator of this game, I was responsible for its art, UI design, music, character design, and over 350,000 words of story content, including a wealth of dialogue and action. It is by far my most comprehensive work and the most recent display of my abilities.

This section of my portfolio showcases some of the art and music present in the game, as well as advertising materials used before and during its release.


 USER INTERFACE

Wyrdwic is a 2D game with a focus on managing the protagonist's guild through menus rather than direct character control or movement. When the player launches the game, they're brought to the "Load" menu (shown on the left), where they can choose from up to three save files. Once a file is loaded, the player is brought to the main menu (shown on the right), where they can start the next week or view unlocked conversations and guardians.

QUEST SELECTION

At the core of Wyrdwic's gameplay is its quest system. In the Weekly Quests menu, the player selects which requests their guild will attempt to complete at the start of each in-game week. Each request has a short letter accompanying it, sent by a character in world, followed by a series of icons that tell the player all they need to know about the selected request. In the example below, the quest's page has icons for the Food and Wealth resources, and an indicator that shows the quest requires the guild to send at least one character to complete it. Hovering the mouse over each of these icons shows a short description of its meaning.

When the player accepts the request, the screen shifts to the right, where they can choose which characters from their guild they wish to send to complete the request. Hovering over each character lists the skills they have, allowing the player to decide who is best suited to completing the given task. Once enough characters have been selected, the "begin" button activates, queuing the quest for completion later in the week and returning the player to the initial screen to continue allocating their characters to the given requests.


QUEST COMPLETION

Once all requests have been accepted or refused, the game proceeds to the next menu, where the player must choose the actions that their guild members take while completing the given requests. Most of these actions require specific skills, and may result in the request's failure if the player chooses the wrong character to act them out. While a character is selected, choices that will be successful are coloured with a bright red while those that will fail are coloured a navy blue.

Each request has a short section describing the events that are taking place, as well as the effects the player's choices are having on the game's world. As the story progresses, the choices the player makes during these requests can have a significant affect on the world, influencing the protagonist on the course of their journey.

EVENTS

The story of Wyrdwic is mostly told through dialogue heavy events at the end of each week of quests, in which player interaction is limited. Clicking the mouse either progresses the dialogue to the next line or activates one of the buttons at the bottom of the screen, or any that appear during narrative choices. The mouse location also moves the foreground and background independently of eachother, creating a parallax effect. This has no direct gameplay effect, but makes this menu, which is less interactive than most in the game, feel more responsive to the player's input.

As for the buttons at the bottom of the screen, "SETTINGS" takes the player to the pause menu (which can also be accessed by pressing the ESC key), "HIDE" removes the text box from the bottom of the screen, the "RECORD" button takes the player to a menu where they can see all dialogue that has been displayed since the game was opened in case they missed anything, and the "INDEX" button takes them to a menu where they can view short descriptions of places, characters and concepts in the world in case they've been forgotten.

PAUSE

The pause menu, accessed with the ESC key, allows the player to change their settings, view current objectives and past tutorials, access the "RECORD" and "INDEX" menus from the event section from anywhere in the game, and return to the main menu where they can load different save files.

OTHERS

While these are the primary menus that the player interacts with while playing Wyrdwic, there are a collection of other menus included in the game. For instance, the "Favours" menu (shown on the left), where the player can use the resources they've gathered to call on other characters in the world for help, and the "Conversations" menu (on the right), where the player can view optional dialogue scenes that show character interactions that don't have a direct impact on the main story.

CONTROLLER SUPPORT

Though the game was originally designed with a mouse in mind, it does include full support for xbox, playstation and nintendo switch controllers, complete with button icons in all the previously shown menus that are only visible while a controller is in active use. In addition, some menus, such as the one shown on the right below, display controller specific tooltips to make up for the fact that the player can't hover over individual elements on the screen.

 CHARACTER AND BACKGROUND ART

CHARACTERS

As the puzzle pieces the player needs to utilise in order to progress through the game's countless quests, the characters are undeniably one of the most important parts of Wyrdwic, each with their own unique personality, appearance, set of skills, and story that reveals itself over the course of the game. 32 of Wyrdwic's characters were given a physical appearance, complete with a handful of visible emotions and a unique colour palette and silhouette so that each could be identified at a glance. ACHIEVEMENTS

A late addition to the game, achievements were included in Wyrdwic's steam release as a way for players to track missable content. Of the 34 achievements in the game, 5 are unavoidable in the course of completing the story, 6 are given for the game's various endings, 12 are given for actions the player can take throughout the story that have a notable affect on Wyrdwic's world, and 6 are given for completing various characters' individual story paths. The remaining 5 achievements are granted for completing challenges in gameplay that have little direct impact on the story.  SOUND AND MUSIC

MUSIC

The 23 musical tracks included in Wyrdwic were created in Cakewalk Sonar using open source instruments included in "VSCO 2 Community Edition" by Versilian Studios. Each is designed as a 30 second to 1 minute long loop that seamlessly repeats until the track changes. Because of the short duration of these looping tracks, the background music changes regularly during events, and often is replaced with silence where applicable in an attempt to ensure it never feels too repetitive. SOUND EFFECTS

As a menu heavy game, there's little in the way of diagetic SFX in Wyrdwic, though to help menus feel responsive and engaging, almost all interactive elements were given sounds that play when used. In addition, scrolling text in dialogue scenes play a short repeating note while the text is being revealed, analogous to speech, with a repeating click for non-spoken text (such as descriptions or internal thoughts) and the repeating chime of a triangle for spoken text. These sound effects were made using the same instrument library as above, usually by modifying the sound of percussion instruments to fit each use case.
